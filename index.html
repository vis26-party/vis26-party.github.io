How

It's about 20 minutes from the Westin Copley, if you take the Orange Line from Back Bay 4 stops to State and then walk for 8 minutes (State to Kilby to Batterymarch).

Find a host from the Tableau, UBC, UW, Utah, or Berkeley research groups, to see if we still have drink tickets left. Otherwise, there will be a cash bar. It’s fine to bring other people along.

What's New!

Papers, awards, and people from the host groups at VIS 26

Tableau

Tableau Research is once again a bronze sponsor at VIS! Srishti Palani, Nicole Sultanum, and Vidya Setlur will be attending, and have a pretty busy schedule this year. Nicole will present Not Always Top-Left: Untangling the Signals that Guide Dashboard Reading Order, and participate as a panelist in the Industry Meets Academia: Data and Visual Analytics in the Agentic Era panel. Srishti will present Lexara-RF: Reference-Free Metrics for Evaluating Conversational Visual Analytics Agents. Vidya is co-organizing the VIS x GenAI workshop and the Beyond the Last Mile: What Should Visualization Own in the Age of Data Systems and AI Agents? panel. We are hiring summer interns, so come say hi!

UBC

Tamara's InfoVis Group at UBC Vancouver will have three PhD candidates at VIS this year. Matt Oddo will present his TVCG paper Visualization Tasks for Unlabeled Graphs. Ryan Smith will present his VIS paper HealthTale: A Patient-Centric Health Story Visualization Tool and participate in the Doctoral Colloquium. New PhD student Joe Hilleary will also attend. Tamara will present the TVCG paper VIVA: Virtual Healthcare Interactions Using Visual Analytics, With Controllability Through Configuration and a tutorial on Visualization Analysis and Design. She is co-editor of the AK Peters Visualization book series with CRC/Routledge, which will have a physical table at VIS. UBC welcomes Andrew McNutt, who will join us as an assistant professor in January 2027.

UW

Jeff and Leilani continue to co-lead the Interactive Data Lab at UW, though Jeff is currently on sabbatical spending time with his start-up Ridge AI. We’re excited to celebrate a 10 Year Test-of-Time Award for Vega-Lite (with alums Arvind Satyanarayan, Dominik Moritz, and Kanit Wongsuphasawat). Medina Lamkin and Heer Patel will co-present their VIS paper What Do Visualization Instructors Want Students to Learn? Introducing a Concept Inventory for Visualization Design.

UC Berkeley

Marti Hearst's group at UC Berkeley is represented at Vis this year by PhD student Jasmine Shih’s research on Two Decades of Community-based Chart Revisions from Wikimedia Commons and Chase Stokes’ (now graduated with his PhD!) VisXVision workshop paper on Visual Embellishments are Potential Distractions in Double-Column Reading (with Cindy Xiong’s student Songwen Hu)

Utah

Utah's Scientific Computing and Imaging Institute brings a whopping 37 research contributions (mixing papers, short papers, journal papers, and posters) spanning topological data analysis, feature-preserving compression, volume rendering, AI and visualization, accessibility, and visualization research methods. These include two main conference paper awards (a best paper for EXaCTz and an honorable mention for Topological Analysis of Kernel Density Estimates), two workshop awards (a best paper at TopoInVis Explainable Reaction Atlas and an honorable mention for LoCoMapper at UncertaintyVis)—all led by Bei Wang and students. In addition Professor Emeritus Chuck Hansen won a 25 year Test of Time award for his work on Interactive volume rendering. We're also running a tutorial on running online user studies with reVISit. Lots of Utah students, postdocs, and PIs will be around, come say hi!

We hope to see you there!
